Why it matters

Sealed containers are everywhere

Food, pharma, chemicals, parcels — the world is full of containers whose contents matter and whose lids vision can't penetrate. A robot that can only look is blind to all of them. Haptic probing turns the arm itself into the sensor.

No new hardware

The sensing channel already exists on every torque-reporting arm. No $5k wrist sensor, no wiring, no extra calibration step. The method is software: a fixed probing motion plus a feature map. That makes it deployable on arms that are already in the field.

Any arm can play

The pipeline's only contract with the robot is a CSV schema — timestamped joint angles and torques with phase tags. Any builder can point their own macro at it (FEELS_MACRO_CMD), keep the extractor and vector memory untouched, and teach their own library. Modularity isn't a slide here; it's the interface.

Beyond the coffee cup

  • Parcel and package sorting: what's inside this box — liquid, fragile, or solid — before the gripper commits.
  • Assistive robotics: a robot handing you a container should know whether it's full, and of what.
  • Warehouse QC: flag a mislabeled or leaking container from its dynamics, inline, without opening it.

The research angle

Most robot perception is vision-first. Humans do something different with a wrapped gift: they shake it. feels is a small, concrete instance of that idea — active haptic perception as a complement to vision, using the excitation-and-listen pattern that works even when the thing you care about is hidden. The cup is the demo; the pattern generalizes.
