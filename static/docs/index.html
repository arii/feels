Can a robot feel what's inside a sealed cup?

feels is a haptic perception system: a robot arm picks up a lidded coffee cup it cannot see into, swirls it once, and identifies the contents — water, soap, candy, a solid weight, or nothing — from joint-torque telemetry alone. No camera looks inside. No force-torque sensor. The swirl becomes a 10-dimensional physical signature, matched by vector search in MongoDB Atlas.

The 30-second version

  1. Probe: pick up → swirl → place → back-off. One fixed, repeatable motion.
  2. Encode: the torque time series becomes a 10-D signature (mass offsets, swirl variances, phase lag, rattle energy…), scaled and L2-normalized to a unit embedding.
  3. Retrieve: MongoDB Atlas $vectorSearch finds the nearest taught signatures by cosine similarity. New items are taught live — no retraining.

Try it: open the dashboard, teach a few items in Mode 2, then identify a mystery cup in Mode 1.
